The supplied file begins with the 7 March 2011 notification and states that the rules came into force on 1 April 2011. It contains the original Rule 5 and Second Schedule, the original Rule 6 date wording, and the original Rule 32 penalty text. Those passages are historical baseline text rather than a reliable statement of the law as it stands in October 2026.  The baseline PDF expressly contains Rule 5 requiring commodities in the Second Schedule to be packed in specified standard quantities, and the Second Schedule lists old fixed quantities for baby food, biscuits, cereals, coffee, tea and other commodities. The 2022 amendment later omitted Rule 5 and the Second Schedule.  The baseline PDF also states in Rule 6(1)(d) that the month and year of manufacture, pre-packing or import must be mentioned. The 2021 amendment removed the words “or pre-packed or imported”, changing the current legal formulation. The baseline PDF likewise has no modern Unit Sale Price rule.  Correct approach:   retain the 2011 document as a historical source/version, but apply a versioned amendment layer with effective dates before making a current PASS/FAIL decision.  Baseline evidence from the supplied file  • Original notification: 7 March 2011; commencement: 1 April 2011.  • Original Rule 5 required commodities in the Second Schedule to be packed in specified standard quantities.  • Original Rule 6(1)(d) required month/year of manufacture, pre-packing or import.  • Original Rule 32 contained fixed   n 4,000 /   n 2,000 rule-contravention fines.  • The Second Schedule lists specified quantities for commodities including baby food, biscuits, cereals, coffee and tea.

DETAILED CURRENT AMENDMENTS  4.1 Unit Sale Price (USP) — Rule 6(11)  The 2021 amendment inserted a Unit Sale Price requirement and the 2022 amendment refined the operative text. The current framework requires a rupee-denominated unit sale price, rounded to two decimal places, using the prescribed unit according to the nature/size of the net quantity: per gram or kilogram for mass, per centimetre or metre for length, per millilitre or litre for volume, and per number/unit where sold by number. Exceptions apply, including where retail sale price equals unit sale price and specified group/combination/multi-piece packages.  AI/compliance implementation:   Calculate USP from declared price/net quantity, compare it with the declared USP, and distinguish mathematical mismatch from missing declaration.  4.2 Removal of Rule 5 and the Second Schedule  The 2021 amendment omitted Rule 5. The 2022 amendment expressly omitted the Second Schedule. The supplied 2011 Second Schedule is therefore historical text, not the current general standard-pack-size mandate.  AI/compliance implementation:   Do not flag a package merely because its quantity is not one of the old Second Schedule sizes. Validate current declarations, USP and any commodity-specific current requirement.  4.3 Country of Origin — physical package  The 2017 amendment inserted Rule 6(1)(aa): the name of the country of origin or manufacture or assembly, in the case of imported products, must be mentioned on the package.  AI/compliance implementation:   OCR should detect and normalize country-of-origin evidence and send ambiguous cases to REVIEW.  4.4 E-commerce — mandatory declarations  The 2017 amendment introduced e-commerce definitions and required the mandatory Rule 6 declarations, except the month/year manufacture-or-packing declaration, to be displayed on the digital/electronic network used for e-commerce transactions.  AI/compliance implementation:   Accept package images and listing evidence separately. Produce distinct physical-label and online-listing compliance results.  4.5 Country-of-Origin filter for e-commerce — 2026  G.S.R. 128(E), 13 February 2026, inserted Rule 6(10A), requiring e-commerce entities selling imported products to provide product listings with a searchable and sortable Country-of-Origin filter. G.S.R. 312(E), 27 April 2026, substituted the provision and states that it applies with effect from 1 July 2027. On 6 October 2026 this is enacted but future-effective.  AI/compliance implementation:   Store effective_from=2027-07-01 and classify it as FUTURE_REQUIREMENT before that date, not as a present statutory FAIL.  4.6 Date declaration simplification — 2021  The 2021 amendment removed “or pre-packed or imported” from Rule 6(1)(d). The current rule set should not be implemented by copying the old 2011 phrase unchanged. Sector-specific regimes and exemptions still need checking.  AI/compliance implementation:   Version the rule text and do not train the compliance model on the old phrase as if it were current.  4.7 QR-code declarations for electronic products — 2023  G.S.R. 456(E), 23 June 2023, amended Rule 6 to permit specified information for electronic products to be provided through a QR code, subject to the package informing consumers to scan the QR code and the information being declared through the QR mechanism rather than on the package itself.  AI/compliance implementation:   Detect QR codes, decode when possible, validate the permitted declaration content, and preserve package image plus decoded evidence.  4.8 AEO Tier-2/Tier-3 bonded warehouses — 2026

PENALTIES AND ENFORCEMENT — CURRENT ACT-LEVEL POSITION  The supplied PDF's Rule 32 contains the old   n 4,000 /   n 2,000 rule-contravention text. That historical text should not be used as the sole penalty engine. The Legal Metrology Act, 2009 has subsequently been amended by Jan Vishwas legislation.  Jan Vishwas 2023:   the Department of Consumer Affairs records that seven sections of the Legal Metrology Act were decriminalized/reworked and that those amendments came into force on 1 October 2023. The effect was to replace selected imprisonment-based consequences with monetary penalties and revised compounding/enforcement provisions.  Jan Vishwas 2026:   the Legal Metrology-related provisions came into force on 1 May 2026. The reform introduced an improvement-notice mechanism for specified first-time lapses, allowing a regulated person an opportunity to rectify covered deficiencies before the later-stage penalty framework applies.  Current Section 36(1) — package declaration non-conformity:   a first covered offence is subject to an improvement notice; the second offence can attract a penalty up to   n 5 lakh; subsequent offences carry a fine of not less than   n 25 lakh and up to   n 50 lakh. The provision expressly covers digital modes such as e-commerce platforms and online marketplaces.  Current Section 36(2) — net-quantity error:   the current text provides a fine of not less than   n 10,000 and up to   n 1 lakh for the first offence; up to   n 5 lakh for the second offence; and up to   n 50 lakh for the third/subsequent offence, or imprisonment up to one year, or both.  System design implication:   your enforcement module should map a detected defect to the current Act section, offence history, effective date and remedy stage. A label defect should not automatically be assigned the old Rule 32   n 2,000 fine. CHAPTER –II  PROVISIONS APPLICABLE TO PACKAGES INTENDED FOR RETAIL SALE  3. Applicability of the Chapter-  The provisions of this Chapter shall not apply to,-  (a) packages of commodities containing quantity of more than 25 kg or 25 litre excluding cement and fertilizer sold in bags up to 50 kg; and  (b) packaged commodities meant for industrial consumers or institutional consumers.  Explanation :- For the purpose of this rule,-  i)   “institutional   consumer”   means   the   institutional   consumer   like   transportation, Airways, Railways, Hotels, Hospitals or any other service institutions who buy packaged commodities directly from the manufacturer for use by that institution.  ii)   “industrial   Consumer”   means   the   industrial   consumer   who   buy   packaged commodities directly from the manufacturer for use by that industry.  4. Regulation for pre-packing and sale etc. of commodities in packaged form. –  On and from the commencement of these rules, no person shall pre-pack or cause or permit to be pre-packed any commodity for sale, distribution or delivery unless the package in which the commodity is pre-packed bears thereon or, a label is securely affixed thereto such declarations as are required to be made under these rules.  Explanation.- The existence of packages without the declaration of retail sale price within the manufacturer’s premises shall not be construed as a violation of these rules and it shall be ensured that all packages leaving the premises of manufacturer for their destination shall have declaration of retail sale price on them as required in these rules.  5.
